**Defer speculative decoding as a priority.** First establish supported-extraction quality and ordinary autoregressive latency for Qwen3-8B with Envoy v5. Speculation can preserve—and accelerate—the existing target distribution; it cannot establish the adapter’s quality benefit.

**Measured evidence.** Two primary papers demonstrate substantial gains under different conditions:

- [Leviathan et al., arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192v2): T5-XXL 11B with T5-small 77M, batch size one, single TPU-v4: **2.3–3.4×** across summarization/translation and sampled/greedy decoding. Their analysis depends on acceptance, cheap drafting and sufficient parallel verification capacity.
- [Chen et al., v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318v1): Chinchilla 70B with a specially designed 4B draft on **16 TPU-v4s**, batch size one: **2–2.5× decoding speedup**. Draft latency was 1.8 versus 14.1 ms/token; acceptance varied by task and decoding settings.

Neither establishes gains for an adapted, potentially quantized 8B reader at concurrency two.

**Conditional calculations.** With three draft tokens, constant independent acceptance \(a\), draft-step cost \(c\) and verification cost \(v\), normalized to one ordinary target step:

\[
S_{\rm decode}\approx\frac{1+a+a^2+a^3}{v+3c}.
\]

At \(v=1,c=0.1\), acceptance 0.8 gives **2.27×**, while 0.3 gives only **1.09×**. These are illustrations, excluding draft prefill and serving overhead. If decoding occupies half of baseline latency, even 2× decoding yields only **1.33× overall**. The 4,000/200 token ratio alone cannot determine that fraction.

Using [Qwen’s configuration](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B/blob/main/config.json), BF16 target KV for two 4,200-token sequences is approximately **1.15 GiB**: \(2·4200·2·36·8·128·2\) bytes. BF16 8.2B weights add roughly **15.3 GiB**, before adapters, draft weights/KV, activations and runtime allocations. Fit on 24GB is plausible, unproven; QLoRA training does not establish inference precision. Drafting and verification compete with both requests on the same GPU.

**Implementation and guarantees.** [vLLM v0.18.0](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.18.0/features/speculative_decoding/draft_model/) documents Qwen3-8B with Qwen3-0.6B and tensor parallelism one. Its [guarantee documentation](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.18.0/features/speculative_decoding/) targets memory-bound, lower-QPS workloads and describes distribution preservation within numerical limits, rather than identical seeded outputs. Verification must use the **adapted target**, unchanged sampling transformations and rejection correction. Changing quantization, thinking mode or acceptance rules requires separate evaluation.

**Unknowns and smallest next experiment.** GPU model, deployment precision, adapter/speculation compatibility, acceptance and prefill/decode split remain unknown. Once baseline quality is acceptable, propose one bounded A/B: 30 representative English-paper extraction prompts, about 4,000/200 tokens, concurrency two; identical target/settings, speculation off versus Qwen3-0.6B with three draft tokens. Record acceptance, drafting/verification time, end-to-end latency, throughput, peak VRAM and evidence-span support. Advance only for a predefined useful gain—say ≥15% lower median latency without material tail-latency, throughput or quality regression.
