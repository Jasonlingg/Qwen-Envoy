**Defer speculative decoding as a priority.** First establish supported-extraction quality and a target-only latency baseline. Speculation preserves the reader’s distribution; it cannot establish Envoy v5’s quality benefit. A later, bounded prompt-lookup test is reasonable without another GPU.

**Measured evidence.** Two foundational papers demonstrate gains under different conditions:

- Leviathan et al. (ICML 2023) measured T5-XXL 11B with a T5-small 77M draft, batch one, single TPU-v4. CNN/DailyMail summarization achieved **2.3× sampled / 3.1× greedy** speedup. Larger drafts improved acceptance but could reduce speed because drafting cost increased. [Paper, Table 2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/leviathan23a/leviathan23a.pdf)
- Chen et al. (2023, v1) measured Chinchilla 70B with a purpose-built 4B draft on 16 TPU-v4s, batch one, four-token lookahead: **1.92× XSum nucleus / 2.46× HumanEval**. Draft latency was 1.8 versus 14.1 ms/token for the target. Neither result predicts Qwen3-8B performance on your GPU. [Paper, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.01318v1)

**Conditional calculations.** Leviathan’s simplified decode-speed model is
\(S=(1+\alpha+\cdots+\alpha^k)/(1+kc)\), where \(k\) is draft length, \(\alpha\) acceptance, and \(c\) draft-token latency relative to a target step. With \(k=3,\alpha=0.8,c=0.1\), it predicts **2.27× decode speedup**, assuming verification costs one ordinary target step. Actual verification, lookup and scheduling overhead can erase this. If decoding accounts for half total latency, even 2× decoding gives only **1.33× overall**. Measure that fraction; 4,000 input versus 200 output tokens does not determine it. [Analysis, §3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/leviathan23a/leviathan23a.pdf)

At two simultaneous 4,200-token sequences, Qwen3’s configuration implies approximately **1.15 GiB target KV cache** at BF16: \(2×4200×36×2×8×128×2\) bytes. This excludes weights, adapter, activations, workspaces and speculative buffers. A learned draft adds weights and usually its own cache; both requests compete for the same GPU. Two-request batching may also reduce speculation’s advantage. QLoRA training does not establish serving precision. [Official configuration, revision 47719a2](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B/blob/47719a242beab8f9aecc40ce3928b034dd5dd559/config.json)

**Guarantee and unknowns.** Correct rejection sampling preserves the **adapted, deployed target’s** distribution, subject to numerics—not identical seeded sampled text. Keep quantization, thinking mode, sampling and stopping fixed. vLLM **v0.10.2** documents prompt n-gram proposals and numerical/batching caveats; its page also warns that learned-draft support was absent in v0.10.0. Exact GPU, runtime, adapter/speculation compatibility, memory headroom, acceptance and prefill share remain unknown. [Implementation documentation](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.10.2/features/spec_decode.html)

**Smallest useful next experiment—proposed only.** After compatibility checks, compare speculation off against **three-token prompt lookup**, on 30–50 representative English extraction prompts, approximately 4,000/200 tokens, two concurrent requests. Copying evidence may favor lookup; that is unmeasured. Hold the deployed target fixed. Record acceptance, verification/drafting time, peak VRAM, TTFT, median/p95 completion latency and supported-extraction accuracy. Adopt only with ≥15% median improvement, no material p95 regression, both requests fitting, and preserved decoding behavior. Otherwise defer learned drafts.
