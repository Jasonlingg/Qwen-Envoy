## Source-linked claims

- ROSS argues historical rollouts contain mistakes and redundant actions that should not all be imitated, and applies supervision selectively. [Abstract, lines 15-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35954)
- Reflective Recovery turns failed prefixes into recovery examples and reports gains on mathematical reasoning benchmarks. [Abstract, lines 16-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19156)
- SCoRe found offline supervised correction traces can suffer distribution mismatch and behavior collapse, so corrected traces are not guaranteed to teach recovery. [Abstract, lines 15-19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12917)

Baseline: Unfiltered teacher-generated successful traces, with a human verifying tool execution and answer evidence before any use.

Cheapest matched test: Use the 100-review budget on 50 reader failure cases and 50 teacher traces sampled from the same question/failure strata. Blindly score each for a novel, replayable, evidence-supported action/answer correction and annotation minutes. No training is required for this screening. If training later becomes available, compare equal-count SFT sets on frozen held-out questions.

Decision rule: Allocate subsequent annotation to corrected failures if at least 10 more of the 50 are novel, replayable, evidence-supported learning examples than the teacher arm, or if yield per annotation hour is at least 25% higher, with no increased rate of unsupported final answers in a later matched SFT test. These are local pilot thresholds.

Uncertainty: No direct head-to-head study on this reader exists. Manual correction may be slow and can introduce annotator inconsistency; teacher traces may help novel tool paths. The cited correction studies concern different models/tasks and do not establish a local effect size.
